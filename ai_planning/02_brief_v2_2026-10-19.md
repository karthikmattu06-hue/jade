# Jade brief v2 — the inference flexibility datasheet (19 Oct 2026, state as of 21 Oct)

*Refined plan, drafted with Claude after my decisions of 9–19 Oct.*

## The decision (19 Oct)
Jade measures the **flexibility envelope of live LLM inference** on a split prefill/decode deployment and reports it in the terms a utility contract uses — the inference counterpart of Emerald AI's training datasheet (Colangelo et al., arXiv:2609.11542), which leaves inference to future work. Rows, per pool (prefill GPU, decode GPU) and per lever:
1. **Depth** — watts removed, down to the loaded-idle floor.
2. **Speed** — time from command to new steady state.
3. **Duration** — how long the cut holds before queueing makes latency unacceptable.
4. **Cost** — p99 TTFT and TPOT during the cut, by request tier.
5. **Release** — power and latency in the 60 s after the limit lifts (the "no snap-back" clause in utility terms).

Levers: admission by tier (reject / defer), drain, replica count (software, SPORC); GPU power cap and SM clock lock (root, rented A100). Definitions: `schema/README.md`.

## Why not the September proposal
Overtaken by PowerSlider (arXiv:2608.21719: time-varying caps, per-stage DVFS + admission on A100s; excludes draining, never measures release), PALS (arXiv:2605.21427: caps + batch size in vLLM, not phase-split) and Yan et al. (arXiv:2609.24639: removing a decode instance on H200s). And "no root" is not a market: the party paid for demand response is the facility operator, who has root.

## Course framing
Python prototype produces Update I numbers. Course deliverable instrument: C++ probe (threads, NVML C API, 20 Hz, ring buffer), C++ load generator (libcurl, thread per stream, open-loop), MPI harness (rank 0 controller issues step changes with `Isend`; one rank per GPU; `Barrier`-aligned windows; `Gather` of samples). The release row is where MPI matters: two power traces and one command log on a shared timebase. CUDA microbenchmark (J/byte, J/FLOP) off the critical path.
